## Answers to the nine questions

**1. How many distinct finished pieces actually exist?**
Fifteen finished narrative Useful Thinking pieces with full copy I can verify, plus at least two more live on the website that I have never seen in full. Separately, the Social Content Library holds thirty-one logged social posts, not all of which are Useful-Thinking-calibre personal narrative — several are shorter positioning or proof posts (Core Gym updates, the company launch post) that shouldn't be counted alongside the fifteen.

**2. How many are live?**
Confirmed live: two, and I don't have their full text. Of my fifteen tracked finished pieces, **zero** have direct evidence of being implemented on the website — only of being handed to the website worker. I was treating "sent to handover" as functionally equivalent to "live" in my last answer. It isn't, and I should have been clearer about that the first time.

**3. How many strong unpublished pieces exist?**
One genuinely promising unfinished draft (Three Nil Up), plus five already-drafted-but-held pieces in my own repo (two needing another Tom pass, three ranked strong-but-later), plus five concrete named-but-unextracted incidents, plus four abstract priority areas with no incident yet. Call it roughly fifteen live threads in some stage of development, none of them mythical — every one is grounded in something specific.

**4. Which pieces are duplicates or materially overlapping?**
The garage story exists in three uncoordinated versions with no canonical one chosen. "Every Rule Changes Behaviour" is independently tracked in two systems. Everything else that touches the same territory (the two £120k pieces, Serendipity/Some People) is a deliberate, approved split, not an accident.

**5. The major strands already well covered**
Using the Map document's own framework, which I independently agree with after reviewing everything: seeing clearly before acting on certainty or hope; structure that controls human variability without pretending to remove judgement; consistency mattering more than the appearance of fairness; the owner's judgement as both asset and bottleneck; protecting the whole business against a locally-sensible decision; growth reducing control before it reduces profit; accountability without personally absorbing everything. Staff loyalty is covered from three angles now (Tightrope, Staff Retention, Some People). Owner-dependency-as-crisis has real, severe evidence (Serendipity).

**6. The major commercial gaps still missing**
This is where the wider evidence base changes the picture most. The Map document's own conclusion — reached independently, before this naming conversation started — is that **TRANSFER is materially weaker** than the other three stages (SEE, STRUCTURE, BALANCE). Serendipity and Some People, both added after that conclusion was written, close part of that gap, but the document's own Priority 2 (losing a non-owner key person, not just risking it) is still open. Beyond that: **execution and diffused responsibility** (Priority 3) has nothing. **Outside perspective and sale readiness** (Priority 4) has nothing, and this is the same exit-story gap flagged at the very start of this whole project. **Contribution and full economic cost, distinct from cash-flow timing** (Priority 5) has nothing. And three things nobody has flagged yet but which jump out from your own category list: **pricing** has no genuine piece anywhere — the only pricing material in the entire estate sits inside the document I already flagged as likely fabricated. **Suppliers**, from Tom's side as the buyer rather than the creditor, has nothing. And **value creation** — proactively building something better, not just avoiding damage — is thin across the whole library; almost everything is about damage avoided or risk managed, which is only half of Arrington's own stated position ("take something that works and make it stronger").

**7. Does this materially change my previous view of the three titles?**
Yes, and in a specific direction. "I Might Be Wrong" is weaker than I first found — the wider set adds more pieces (Turning, the shipping story, Every Rule) that don't fit an error/certainty frame, not fewer. "Tested Judgement" fits the SEE/STRUCTURE/BALANCE material reasonably well, but that's exactly the problem: it fits what exists, and what exists is missing entire territory the library's own roadmap already plans to build (transfer, execution, outside perspective, value creation). A title landed on now, based on today's damage-avoidance-heavy fourteen or fifteen pieces, carries real risk of being the "I Might Be Wrong" problem all over again in six months — accurate for tranche one, straining against tranche two.

**8. Should we develop the missing thinking before naming the page?**
Yes. Not as a stalling tactic — because your own Map document already reached the same conclusion independently, before this naming exercise existed: "Do not write article 14 at random. Build the next piece around Priority 1." The business's own roadmap already says the library is mid-build, specifically in the direction (transfer, execution, value creation) most likely to stress-test any title chosen today. Naming now optimises for a snapshot; the roadmap says the snapshot is about to change shape.

**9. Revisit naming only if justified**
On this evidence, not yet. Not because "Useful Thinking" or "Tested Judgement" are wrong — because there isn't enough of the real shape yet to choose with confidence, and the cost of waiting is low. Nothing about the page has actually changed; the switching cost concern from the original pressure-test still applies whenever the decision is made. The lower-risk move is what I recommended last time regardless — Useful Thinking stays as the live label, and naming gets revisited once Priority 1 or 2 produces a real TRANSFER piece, since that's the stage most likely to reveal whether any candidate title actually holds.

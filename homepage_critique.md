# Creative Director Critique — WSA Homepage

## Reviewed Against
- Does this immediately feel like a premium international education company?
- Does it build more trust than the current WSA website?
- Does it feel comparable in quality to Paradise Education?
- Is there anything that still feels generic?
- Is there anywhere the layout feels crowded?
- Is there anywhere the visitor has to think too hard?
- Is the Personal Student Counsellor clearly the biggest differentiator?
- Does the homepage create aspiration as well as trust?
- Would a parent trust WSA within five seconds?
- Would a student immediately understand the next step?

---

## The Ten Biggest Weaknesses

### 1. The page is too long and repetitive
The homepage has 10 sections and repeats the counsellor message in at least 5 of them. By the time a visitor reaches section 7, they've read "one named counsellor" multiple times. This dilutes impact. Paradise says something once, powerfully. WSA says it five times, weakly. The page needs to be shorter and every section needs to say something NEW.

### 2. The "Our Team" section with placeholder boxes is actively damaging
Grey boxes with single letters don't build trust — they destroy it. They signal "this company doesn't have real people to show you." A premium brand would never show placeholder content. This section should either be removed entirely until real photos are available, or reimagined without requiring portraits.

### 3. The counsellor section (Section 3) is too text-heavy
It's the most important section on the page, but it reads like a brochure. Three paragraphs + a bulleted list + a "For parents" block + a CTA link = too much to absorb. The visitor's eye has nowhere to rest. Paradise communicates equivalent information with one sentence and one image. The content is right but the presentation is overwhelming.

### 4. The "Why families choose WSA" section is a wall of text on a dark background
Five items, each with a heading and paragraph, stacked vertically on a navy background. This is the manifesto section — it should feel bold and confident. Instead it feels like a FAQ page. The left column (philosophy) and right column (proof points) compete for attention. A visitor scrolling quickly will skip this entirely.

### 5. The journey section is functional but not aspirational
Five numbered steps in a list. This is clear and informative, but it doesn't create any emotion. It reads like a process diagram, not a story. Paradise would make this feel like an adventure. WSA makes it feel like a checklist. The image on the right helps, but it's hidden on mobile.

### 6. The trust strip is too quiet
Four small text items in a single row. On first visit, a parent's eye goes straight from the hero to the counsellor section — the trust strip is so visually quiet it gets skipped. The most important trust signal (British Council) deserves more presence. It's currently the same visual weight as "Since 2012."

### 7. The "For Parents" section (graduation image overlay) is easy to miss
Text overlaid on an image with a gradient. On mobile, this will be hard to read. The content is powerful ("We know this decision isn't just about education. It's about trust.") but the presentation is risky — it depends entirely on the image brightness. Also, parent reassurance appears in THREE places (counsellor section, this section, and the CTA). Pick one and make it unforgettable.

### 8. The signature moment ("The First Call Home") lacks visual power
The concept is brilliant and unique. But the execution is: navy background → text → image below. The image is cropped to an extreme aspect ratio (21:9) which makes it feel like a banner, not a moment. This should be the most emotionally powerful section on the page. Currently it's visually weaker than the destinations section.

### 9. Two CTAs to "/contact" appear before the visitor has enough context
The hero has "Apply Now" and the journey section has "Start your application" — both appear before the visitor has seen destinations, reasons to choose WSA, or the team. A parent who hasn't yet decided to trust WSA will not click "Apply Now." The early CTAs create pressure rather than invitation. The final CTA is well-positioned, but the early ones feel premature.

### 10. The page doesn't breathe enough between sections
Despite generous padding (py-28 to py-40), the sections feel dense because each one contains a lot of content. The issue isn't spacing between sections — it's density within them. Each section tries to say too much. The result: the page feels informative but not calm. A truly premium page would say less per section and let each idea land with more space around it.

---

## Summary

The homepage is well-structured, well-intentioned, and strategically sound. But it's trying too hard. It's over-explaining, over-proving, and over-repeating. The result is a page that feels like a very good education consultancy website — not the Apple of international education.

The fix is not to add more. It's to subtract. Say less. Show more. Trust the visitor to understand. Let each section do ONE thing brilliantly rather than three things adequately.

---

## Redesign Principles

1. **Cut the page from 10 sections to 7.** Remove the team placeholder section entirely. Merge the "For Parents" overlay into the counsellor section. Consolidate repeated counsellor messaging.

2. **One idea per section.** Each section gets ONE message, ONE visual, ONE action. No section should require scrolling within itself to absorb.

3. **Make "The First Call Home" the emotional centrepiece.** Larger image. More space. Less text. Let the image do the work.

4. **Reduce text density by 40%.** Every paragraph that can become one sentence should become one sentence. Every bullet list that can become a statement should become a statement.

5. **Move the first CTA down.** The hero should have "Explore Study Options" as primary (low commitment) and "Apply Now" should only appear after trust is established.

6. **Make the trust strip more assertive.** British Council certification should be visually prominent — not just text.

7. **Redesign "Why WSA" as a single powerful statement** rather than five proof points. The manifesto approach is right, but it needs to be shorter and bolder.
